## What it is

Counting calories is tedious, and it's easy to give up on. Plates asks two quick questions
instead. After a meal, you tap **+**, name it, and rate how full you are and how healthy the plate
was. A snack only gets the health rating.

- **How full are you?** A 1 to 10 scale, from "Starving" to "Extremely stuffed". The goal is 6 or
  7: satisfied, not stuffed.
- **How healthy was it?** A 1 to 5 plate check, from "Treat" to "Ideal". Each level describes
  something you can see on the plate, like "half the plate veg, plus protein", so a 4 means the
  same thing next month as it does today.

The timeline shows every meal under its day, with that day's averages. Trends shows how often
meals land in range, and how that changes over a week, a month or three months.

## How it's built

- **Food icons instead of photos.** Each meal gets an icon, like a fish, a salad leaf or a pizza
  slice, picked from the meal's name by a small model on Cloudflare Workers AI. Only the name is
  sent, with nothing saying whose it is, and a name you've eaten before reuses its icon.
- **Scales grounded in research.** Fullness uses the wording of UC Berkeley's Hunger-Satiety
  Scale. The health check draws on the NOVA food-processing groups and Harvard's Healthy Eating
  Plate.
- **Encrypted sync.** Like [Notebook](/projects/notebook), Plates runs on
  [Starling](/projects/starling), so meals are saved on your device first and encrypted before they
  sync.
- **On the web.** It installs from the browser on any phone or computer.
