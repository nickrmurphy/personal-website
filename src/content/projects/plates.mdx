Counting calories is tedious, and it's easy to give up on. Plates is for anyone who wants to eat
better without doing the math: after each meal, you answer two quick questions instead. I design
and build it myself.

You tap **+**, name the meal, and rate how full you are, from 1 ("Starving") to 10 ("Extremely
stuffed"), aiming for 6 or 7. Then you rate how healthy the plate was, from 1 ("Treat") to 5
("Ideal"). Each health level describes something you can see on the plate, like "half the plate
veg, plus protein", so a 4 means the same thing next month as it does today. A snack only gets the
health rating. The timeline shows every meal under its day, and Trends shows how often meals land
in range over a week, a month or three months.

## How it works

Meals are saved to a database on your phone, so logging is instant and works offline. In the
background they sync through [Starling](/projects/starling), encrypted on the device first, just
like [Notebook](/projects/notebook). Instead of a photo, each meal gets an icon, like a fish, a
salad leaf or a pizza slice. A small AI model on Cloudflare picks it from the meal's name, and it
can only answer with one of the icons. Only the name is sent, with nothing saying whose it is, and
a name you've eaten before reuses its icon.

The scales come from research, not my own guesses: fullness uses the wording of UC Berkeley's
Hunger-Satiety Scale, and the health check draws on the NOVA food-processing groups and Harvard's
Healthy Eating Plate. Your meals are yours to take with you, too: Settings exports every one to a
single file and imports it back.

It's an iPhone app, built with [Tauri](https://tauri.app).
